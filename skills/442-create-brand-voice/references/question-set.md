# Question set

One question per message, each with your recommendation inside it, so the team can
say "yes" or correct you. `you decide` is a real answer and is final.

Three questions in the default mode. Seven in `detailed`. Everything not asked, you
decide and write down with a reason.

## Default mode: the three that change the most

### 1. Does it say "you", or "the user"?

Recommendation to offer: second person everywhere, contractions allowed, including
in errors. Give the reason, because it is the one people argue with: an interface
that says "the user must enter a date" is a form talking about somebody who is not
in the room.

What it sets: person, contractions, and how errors are phrased.

### 2. How much does it explain?

The honest input is how often the audience sees this screen. A tool used daily by the
same people can say `Saved.` A form filled once a year has to say what was saved and
where it went.

Recommendation to offer: pick one position on the compressed-to-explanatory dial,
justified by frequency, and hold it across every surface. Most teams write
explanatory copy on the screen they see fifty times a day, which is the mistake this
question exists to prevent.

What it sets: sentence budget, whether empty states teach, whether errors explain
the rule or just the fix.

### 3. What does it refuse to say?

The most useful question in the set. Ask for three words the product would never use.
Teams have strong, correct instincts ("we would never say leverage") and it gives the
banned list real owners instead of an imported word list nobody believes.

Recommendation to offer: two words from the usual offenders (seamlessly, empower,
simply, delight) plus one from their own domain, and check them back.

What it sets: the banned list, and by contrast the words the product does use.

## Detailed mode: four more

### 4. Does it ever joke, and does it ever apologize?

Two separate decisions and the answers are usually different. Recommendation to
offer: apologize by stating what happened and what to do, never by emoting. Allow
humor only where nothing is at stake, and never in an error, a destructive
confirmation, or anything about money.

### 5. Capitalization and punctuation

Recommendation to offer: sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings.
No exclamation marks. One period per sentence. No ellipses except a real loading
state. Say why: Title Case reads as a brochure and exclamation marks read as a
newsletter.

### 6. How are dates, times, and numbers written?

Recommendation to offer: 24 hour times, numerals for all data, a consistent date
format named explicitly, and tabular numerals anywhere a number sits in a column.
This one is dull and it is where interfaces most often look assembled from parts.

### 7. What does onboarding sound like?

Recommendation to offer: one instruction at a time, in the imperative, with the
visible thing named. `Tap a row to see the hours behind it.` Never a tour that
explains the product's philosophy before the person has done anything.

## What to do with the answers

Keep a running list in the conversation, one line per decision, with the reason:

```text
Person: second person, contractions, including errors.
Explanation: compressed. The rota is opened daily by the same supervisor.
Refuses: leverage, empower, "synergy".
Humor: none. Nothing here is low stakes.
Case: sentence case, no exclamation marks.
Dates: 24 hour, DD Mon, tabular numerals in columns.
```

Every line becomes a section of `voice.md`. A line with no reason is not a decision
yet, and it will be the first one a teammate overrules.
