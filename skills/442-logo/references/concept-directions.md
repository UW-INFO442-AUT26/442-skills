# Concept directions

The thinking that happens before a prompt is written. Two tables and one rule.

## Character to geometry

Pick one character word in step 3, then take the geometry it implies. This table
is the thing that stops every project getting the same crescent.

| Character | Geometry that carries it | Wording for the prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Steady, dependable | equal weights, symmetry, few angles | perfectly symmetrical, every stroke the same thickness, three or four shapes at most |
| Fast, forward | lean, taper, one long axis | a single form leaning slightly forward, tapering to a point at the leading edge |
| Gentle, caring | large radii, no points, generous space | generous radii, no sharp points anywhere, one wide smooth curve carrying the form |
| Precise, technical | 60 or 90 degree construction, shared tangents | built on a 60 degree grid, all tangents shared, segments of equal length |
| Playful | one deliberate asymmetry | balanced except for one element scaled up, no other variation |
| Growth, progress | an opening gap, an ascending curve | a closed form with one gap widening upward, the thicker end at the top |
| Connection, sharing | shared strokes, one continuous line | two forms sharing a single stroke, drawn as one continuous line of constant weight |
| Protection, safety | an enclosing form with one break | one outline enclosing a single solid core, one clean gap in the outline |
| Clarity, focus | negative space, one shape removed from another | one solid mass with one smooth cut removing a third of it |
| Craft, human | slight irregularity, hand-placed weight | soft organic curves, one slight asymmetry, a confident hand-drawn line |

Two rules for using it:

1. **Translate, do not quote.** The right column is geometry. Never put the left
   column in a prompt: "dependable" gives you a shield, "growth" gives you an
   arrow and a leaf, and both are stock icons.
2. **One word wins.** If two character words are true, run them as two separate
   directions rather than mixing them into one prompt. A contradictory brief
   becomes mush.

## The seven families

Choose three different families for the set. Repeating a family means the student
is choosing between synonyms.

| Family | What it does | Geometry to write |
|---|---|---|
| Object | The thing the product replaces or acts on | a key head reduced to one solid shape |
| Transformation | Two states in one form; the story is the join | a rectangle whose right edge becomes a circle |
| Implied form | Parts the eye completes as a whole | three thick arcs arranged so the gap reads as a circle |
| Letterform | An initial, or a letter built from the product's shape | the letter C drawn as one thick crescent, counter left open |
| Diagram | The product's mechanism: node and edge, cycle, filter, stack | three nodes on one shared stroke, the middle node solid |
| Container | A frame: shield, badge, aperture, ring with a break | a rounded square with one corner cut away |
| Motion | A single stroke with implied direction or spin | one stroke with a heavy head and a thinning tail, curving a quarter turn |

Letterform needs a name that exists. Diagram is the most underused: for software
the product *is* a mechanism, so drawing the mechanism is both distinctive and
honest about what the thing does.

## Matching the mark to the name

If the name is already chosen, its sound should agree with the mark. A hard name
(K, T, P, X) under a soft round mark reads as two different brands.

Sound symbolism is tested and real: people rate `mal` as bigger than `mil`, match
`takete` to spiky shapes and `maluma` to round ones, and the effect survives across
languages. Plosives and hard c read decisive; sibilants read fast; liquids and
nasals read soft.

Full treatment of sound belongs to naming, so if the name is still open, stop here
and Call the Skill tool with `442-create-names` first.
