# What a UX researcher asks before making personas

A researcher does not open a blank document and start inventing people.
They sit with the person who asked for the personas and settle five things
first. Each answer changes the output, which is why the questions come
before any writing.

Ask these in the student's own conversation, one at a time, and take a
short answer. Do not read the whole list out loud.

## A. What will these personas decide?

- Which decision is stuck right now, and who is stuck on it?
- What will you do differently after reading these that you would not do
  now?
- Who else reads them: teammates, the instructor, a future you?

**Why it changes the output:** personas written to settle "should we build
the reminder feature" look different from personas written to settle "who
do we interview next". The first needs stances on one feature. The second
needs a spread of people you have not met.

## B. What do we actually know?

- Who have you talked to, and how many?
- What did they tell you that surprised you?
- What are you assuming because it seems obvious?
- Whose voice is missing from what you have?

**Why it changes the output:** this is the difference between a persona
grounded in three conversations and a persona grounded in a guess. Both
are allowed. Only one may be presented as research.

## C. Who is out of scope?

- Who is this product not for, at least this quarter?
- Which of these people would never be allowed to use it, or would never
  be in the room when it is bought?
- Which edge case are you deliberately ignoring?

**Why it changes the output:** a cast with no exclusions is a cast that
describes everyone, which means it guides nothing. Naming who is out is
how the personas get sharp.

## D. What does the team already believe?

- What is the team's current story about the user, in one sentence?
- Which part of that story would you like these personas to test?
- What would have to be true for you to change your mind?

**Why it changes the output:** personas are a way to argue with the
team's assumptions. If you do not write the assumption down, the personas
quietly agree with it and add nothing.

## E. How will we know they were worth it?

- What decision should be different in two weeks because of these?
- What would make you throw them away?

**Why it changes the output:** this is the check at the end. A persona
set nobody used was a writing exercise.

## What to do with the answers

Keep them. They become the short "why these personas" block at the top of
`PERSONAS.md`, and they decide three things downstream:

1. How many personas. One decision with one user and one approver needs
   three. A product that crosses a handoff needs five.
2. Which tension the cast is built around.
3. How much of each profile is evidence and how much is a labeled guess.

When a student cannot answer A, stop. Personas without a decision are
decorative, and it is cheaper to say so now than after the writing.
